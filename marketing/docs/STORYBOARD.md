# Petty Trailer Storyboard

## Creative Direction

Lead with a real character moving on a clean Mac desktop. Show the product
before explaining it. The primary story is:

> Choose a companion, place it on your desktop, and watch it react while you
> work.

The **30-second launch trailer** is the recommended master storyboard. The
10-second teaser and 60-second demo reuse the same real recorded takes.

| 0:05.5-0:07.5 | Size slider moves from 75% through 100% to 135% | `MAKE IT YOUR SIZE` | Hold each size briefly; three light slider ticks |
| 0:07.5-0:09 | Character click triggers poke animation, then two fast character-selection match cuts | `CHOOSE. MOVE. REACT.` | Let poke reaction read before montage; two short whooshes |

Teaser constraints:

- No more than three characters.
- No sleep demonstration; the real idle threshold cannot be explained clearly
  in this duration.
- The character must be visible within the first second.

## Shot Continuity

Use **Graveyard Intern** as the primary demonstration character because its
idle, walk, attack, and sleepy states read clearly. Keep these consistent:

- Start at 100% size.
- Default lower-right position.
- Same wallpaper and display scaling.
- Same cursor size and recording frame rate.
- Same settings-window position for store and resize shots.

Use other characters only for the selection and ending montage.

## Landscape Composition

- Keep the floating character in the lower-right or right-center area.
- Leave left-side negative space for overlays.
- Place settings left or center so the character remains separately visible.
- During dragging, move horizontally across the lower third.
- Keep cursor highlights smaller than the character.

## Square Composition

- Crop store shots tighter around the Character Store grid.
- Keep the floating character within the center-right quadrant.
- Break long overlays into two balanced lines.
- Avoid placing text along the bottom 15% of the frame.

## Vertical Composition

- Keep Petty within the central 60% of the 1080x1920 canvas.
- Reframe store footage tightly enough to read two character cards at a time.
- Put overlays above the character when it sits in the lower half.
- Do not place critical text at the very top or bottom.
- Reframe each shot manually; the settings window and floating character are
  separate windows, so automatic reframing may follow the wrong subject.

## Edit Notes

- Every app interaction must originate from a real screen recording.
- It is acceptable to cut between separate real takes for pacing.
- Do not composite a character into a desktop where it was not recorded.
- Do not replace the app UI with a mockup.
- Do not accelerate the poke, drag response, or sleep transition to imply
  behavior that did not occur.
- The idle take may be trimmed to its final seconds, but production notes should
  retain the full source recording proving the real threshold.
- Use only the current app's names and controls.
- End on a character, not on a feature checklist.
